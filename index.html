01 / AboutA little context

Data. Health.
The people in between.

I’m Gent, a researcher working at the intersection of computer science and digital health.

My work turns signals from everyday devices into measures that can help us understand health. I combine signal processing, statistics, and machine learning with a close interest in how people use and interpret these technologies.

I completed my PhD in Computer Science at Malmö University in 2026. Much of my research has focused on Parkinson’s disease: measuring symptoms at home, evaluating digital tools, and connecting sensor data with clinical assessment.

I’m interested in methods that travel beyond a single condition, and in research that brings technical rigour together with the realities of everyday life.

02 / ResearchFrom signals to understanding

Health, outside
the clinic.

How can the devices we already carry help us understand the changes that clinical visits may miss?

01

Digital biomarkers

Extracting measures of movement and symptoms from smartphone and wearable sensors, then evaluating them against reference devices and clinical ratings.

Signal processing / Validation
02

Machine learning

Building analysis pipelines that connect real-world sensor data with symptom severity, with attention to participant-level validation and interpretable results.

Time series / Statistical modelling
03

Human-centred health

Studying usability, lived experience, and data interpretation so that symptom-tracking tools make sense to the people living with a condition and those providing care.

User experience / Co-interpretation
